Procreate vs Procreate Dreams: What Each One Is Actually For

By Massoda  •  Tools  •  2026

People ask me this a lot, usually while looking at a piece I've posted and trying to figure out which app to buy. Short answer: they're not competing apps, they do different jobs, and most of my finished creative work actually passes through both.

Procreate: Built for Drawing and Painting

Procreate is where a piece starts and, for illustration work, usually where it finishes. It's a raster painting app — layers, brushes, blending, colour work, all built around a single still image. Every character portrait, digital painting, and static logo mark I make starts here. If your goal is a finished illustration, a painting, or a static design, Procreate is the whole toolkit.

Procreate Dreams: Built for Motion

Procreate Dreams is a separate app built specifically for animation — timelines, keyframes, onion skinning, frame-by-frame drawing tools. It's not a replacement for Procreate; it's what you reach for once a piece needs to move. The Bpétale logo timelapse and the short character motion studies in my sketchbook were built in Dreams, not Procreate.

How They Actually Work Together

In practice, a lot of my animation work starts as a Procreate file — a character design, a logo mark — that gets imported into Dreams once it's time to add motion. Static design decisions (colour, shape, detail) get locked in in Procreate, where the painting tools are stronger; movement decisions happen in Dreams, where the timeline tools are built for exactly that.

Which One Should You Learn First?

If you're deciding where to start: Procreate first, always. Strong drawing and design fundamentals transfer directly into animation, but the reverse isn't true — animating a weak drawing just makes a weak drawing move. Once you're comfortable finishing pieces in Procreate, Dreams becomes a natural next step rather than a steep new mountain.

Both apps live on the same device, which keeps the whole pipeline — sketch, illustrate, animate, export — inside one ecosystem without hopping between a desktop suite and a tablet. For independent creative work, that's a genuinely big deal.
