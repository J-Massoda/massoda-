Engineering judgment

Technical Decision Library

Good software is shaped by the decisions behind it. These short explanations connect common engineering problems to practical examples from my projects and writing.

Why do payment retries need idempotency?

A timeout does not always mean a payment request failed. Idempotency allows safe retries without turning one customer action into multiple financial operations.

Explore ReconLab →

Why should offline applications use a durable outbox?

A visible interface does not prove data reached the server. A durable outbox preserves the local action and the intention to synchronize it later.

Explore KioskFlow →

Why should AI approvals expire?

A human approval should authorize one reviewed action, not give an agent permanent access. Narrow, short-lived grants reduce the effect of mistakes or compromised sessions.

Explore Sentinel →

Why is one API request not enough for fulfilment?

Providers can time out, return ambiguous responses or process a request late. Reliable fulfilment needs persistence, idempotency, bounded retries, logs and operator recovery.

Explore RelayKit →

What do delivery metrics actually tell a team?

Deployment frequency and recovery time reveal system flow. They become harmful when treated as individual scores instead of evidence for improving delivery.

Explore PulseDeck →Read the CI/CD article →

Why verify AI-generated code against the real system?

AI suggestions can sound convincing while naming hooks or configuration keys that do not exist. Source code, documentation, tests and runtime behavior remain the final evidence.
